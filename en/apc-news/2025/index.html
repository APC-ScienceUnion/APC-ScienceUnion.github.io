Happy 9th Birthday to Gewu Society/APC Science Union!

Today marks the ninth anniversary of APC Science Union, also known as Gewu Society. Let us wish the Union a very happy birthday!

Nine summers ago, two students fresh from their high school entrance exams were preparing to begin high school. Their shared passion for science communication brought them together, and they raised the sails of APC Science Union. On the fourth anniversary, members voted to give the Union its official Chinese name, “Gewu Society,” expressing our lasting commitment to the ideal of investigating things in order to gain knowledge.

Nine years later, we remain committed to that original mission. We have now published more than 200 original science articles and nearly 2000 illustrated science posts spanning the natural sciences, social sciences, engineering, and technology, creating a growing library of science communication. Of these, 17 articles have received high praise from organizations including the Chinese Academy of Sciences.

Every article and every act of science communication reflects the hard work of Union members. We offer our heartfelt thanks to everyone who has shared their enthusiasm and given their time so generously. Your commitment has carried Gewu Society to where it is today. We also thank every reader who supports us; your recognition is our greatest motivation to keep going.

One final plug for the 9th-anniversary activities in our group! If our work interests you, please watch for APC’s summer recruitment event. Whether you are an experienced science communicator or simply love learning, taking part will earn you a limited-edition gift. We look forward to meeting more people who share our interests and writing the next chapter of science communication together!

Only 4.6% of AAAI papers are selected for oral presentation, making this a particularly notable achievement in artificial intelligence.

DBLM: Spatiotemporal Resource Management via Deep Black-Litterman Model
– Rethinking spatiotemporal resource management: the DBLM framework for supply-chain optimization

Balancing supply and demand and allocating resources in a complex, changing environment is a central problem in modern supply-chain management. Time Series Supplier Allocation (TSSA) is a representative example. It is recognized as an NP-hard problem: businesses must balance supply with demand when scheduling future orders while minimizing the risk of shortages. Traditional methods, however, often struggle when supplier capacity changes over time or when the available data is unreliable.

A New Approach: DBLM

To address these challenges, we developed the Deep Black-Litterman Model (DBLM), which brings the classic Black-Litterman (BL) model from finance into supply-chain management. DBLM removes the traditional dependence on manually constructed view matrices while addressing missing data and complex spatiotemporal dynamics.

Three core features of DBLM:

  1. Spatiotemporal graph neural networks (STGNNs): These networks dynamically capture changes over time and spatial relationships among suppliers, automatically generating future view matrices and substantially improving predictive accuracy and efficiency.
  2. Ranking optimization: A supervisory signal based on Spearman rank correlation keeps allocation weights consistently ordered with supplier risk, allowing the model to identify low-risk, high-value suppliers more precisely.
  3. Masking: A purpose-built masking mechanism prevents unreliable samples from distorting the model’s predictions, improving robustness and reliability when data quality is uneven.

Experimental Results: Stronger Performance

Experiments on two real-world datasets show that DBLM significantly outperforms existing methods:

  • DBLM raises Hit Ratio@50 to 48.7% on the MCM dataset and 66.2% on the SZ dataset.
  • Ablation studies further confirm the central roles of the spatiotemporal graph neural network and ranking-optimization mechanism.

Open Code and Resources

To support further research, we have released the DBLM code and related resources on GitHub. Researchers and industry practitioners alike can explore how the framework approaches supply-chain optimization.

DBLM moves beyond several limitations of traditional supply-chain optimization and offers a new way to address complex, dynamic resource-allocation problems. Follow our latest work and join the discussion about the future of supply-chain optimization!
