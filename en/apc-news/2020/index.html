Scientist Cosplay No. 3: Linnaeus

A butterfly crosses a hundred years, one wing skimming a vast sea. A black swan rises with the wind; a falling feather brushes a gray rhinoceros’s hoof. This is a story spanning centuries, the universe, and chaos theory, and the third installment in our Scientist Cosplay series.
Text: Sirius King
On camera: Sirius King, Weilong
Makeup: Xunsheng
Props: San
Wigs: Chazi, Bai Lang (I was so busy that day I forgot to bring one. Sorry.)
Photography: Cold Moon Lone Star
Studio: Yaoxiao
Behind-the-scenes photography: Yuxuan, Zhonglin
Logistics: Zhonglin, Yuxuan, Xunsheng, plus two people I could not tag as friends: @TZ and an unnamed young woman
European history consultant: Herr von Schnelltenberg
Costume design team: Sirius King, Weilong, Feibao
Post-production: Song Ziming (our dedicated editor, whose good deeds are beyond measure and who is the loveliest person!)
Story discussion: Omshirogua, Cold Moon Lone Star
Special thanks: Weilong, Millisecond, and the official account. Thanks to the E.A.K. and APC platforms.
Assistance: Uncle Yu, Yaoxiao, Huhu, Linglan, Brother Gou, Baishui, Lu Qing, Su Chen, Lele, Fengge, Fan_GG, kviio
Also: I designed the T-shirt myself, and the pencil case came from Guokr.
~~~~~ Divider below ~~~~~
First, I owe everyone an apology. This set was produced in a rush even though the full process took a very long time. If I have left anyone out of the credits above, my memory simply failed me. I am truly sorry.
I am now in the first semester of my senior year. Last year, I planned to make one set a year, but when the pandemic began this year, I started with Wu Lien-teh. /crying with laughter Then, after more than six unsettled months of the domestic outbreak, much of my equipment remained at school while the campus would not let us return. There was still no word during online classes about reopening in September, so I decided sooner was better and hurriedly launched the project. As for when it began… the concept sheet was already hanging on my wall during the finished Darwin shoot. /crying with laughter The time-travel idea came from Season 5, Episode 10 of Doctor Who. Because Van Gogh’s clothing in that episode does not match his original period, I realized I could use a similar device for an outdoor shoot.
Despite all the preparation, major problems still arose. I especially want to thank Zhonglin, @Jiangling Juejue, who ran around helping throughout the entire production, made countless decisions with me, and taught me a great deal. His contribution was enormous!
Many things went wrong on the day of the shoot. I forgot the wig for young Linnaeus. Bai Lang worked so hard, and I am deeply sorry, @wBaiw. I had originally wanted Linnaeus to leave home as a young man, skip his Lapland expedition, and travel into the future. Instead, the older Linnaeus made the trip. /covers face Thank you to Huhu, @Huhu Makeup, Colored Contacts in Stock 0–800 Degrees, for solving the cravat problem, and to Uncle Yu, @Misty Rain, for tying it. @Yaoxiaoxiao Wants Something for Nothing, I am sorry for what I impulsively did to your door. /covers face/covers face/covers face I could have chosen a better outdoor location. Thank you all for making the long trip with me. I still lacked experience, and I will improve next time.
Now for the behind-the-scenes material. I did try to introduce the specimens in the style of Wuqiong Xiaoliang. I had even prepared carrots, rutabagas, and potatoes, but I had not memorized the script well enough and made too many errors in their families, genera, and species. I was too lazy to reshoot and rebuild the segment, but I could not publish misinformation, so I am sorry to disappoint you both: @Ember All-Purpose Oil (Super Freaking Cute Miku Edition) and @Jiangling Juejue. Yuxuan filmed some behind-the-scenes footage, and I included the Douyin code directly. Scan it if you are interested.
Over the past two days, I have been most grateful to my friends at Gewu Society, especially the official account @Gewu Society | General Science official and Millisecond @Physics Group—One Millisecond of Eternity. Even while handling serious work every day, you kept the spark alive. /covers face Thank you for accepting and supporting me despite how busy you were. Thank you, thank you.
Thank you, everyone! Much love, Lao K.

Scientist Cosplay No. 2: Wu Lien-teh

Light still shines, but darkness is never far away. At Christmas, Santa Claus cannot save lives; modern medicine can.
Wu Lien-teh: Sirius King
Photography: @Dabing Images
Logistics: @Nainai @Xunsheng
Consultants: @Queen Kadula @Imperial Annals @Drache im Herzen @1Barbieee @Zhiai
CC: APC Science Union

At the end of 1910, plague broke out in Harbin. Countless people died, and the three northeastern provinces fell into terror. The Qing court sent Dr. Wu Lien-teh to Harbin. Defying taboos, he called for cremation and was the first to propose the concept of “pneumonic plague.” In four months, he completely ended a once-in-a-century epidemic and saved countless lives, earning the name “plague fighter.” It was the first successful large-scale containment of plague in human history. His work brought him prominence at the International Plague Conference, and in 1935 he was nominated for the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine. The accompanying image shows mortality statistics after the outbreak.
Note: Syringes of this kind did not exist at the time, and neither did streptomycin. Plague was incurable then. Today it remains a Class A infectious disease, but effective drugs can save patients. Two cases of plague were unexpectedly confirmed in Beijing this year, which is what brought Wu to mind. The plague virus has not been eradicated, so remain cautious.
