# Liftoff

&emsp;&emsp;At 12:11 p.m. on August 17, 2019, the Smart Dragon-1 (SD-1) carrier rocket lifted off successfully from the Jiuquan Satellite Launch Center, placing three satellites into their planned orbits in a “one rocket, three satellites” mission. This was China's first commercial space launch conducted entirely under a commercial model, and its success opened a new chapter in the country's commercial space sector.

<img src="/images/“捷龙一号”首飞成功的背后——18个月风雨兼程/640.gif" />

&emsp;&emsp;Recent developments show that China's commercial space sector is beginning to take off. One month before Smart Dragon-1's maiden flight, iSpace became the first private Chinese company to place a satellite into its planned orbit. Just a week before the Smart Dragon launch, another Chinese company, LinkSpace, conducted a test flight and successfully recovered its rocket.

<img src="/images/%E2%80%9C%E6%8D%B7%E9%BE%99%E4%B8%80%E5%8F%B7%E2%80%9D%E9%A6%96%E9%A3%9E%E6%88%90%E5%8A%9F%E7%9A%84%E8%83%8C%E5%90%8E%E2%80%94%E2%80%9418%E4%B8%AA%E6%9C%88%E9%A3%8E%E9%9B%A8%E5%85%BC%E7%A8%8B/cover-15ebdc762b.jpg" title="The Smart Dragon-1 carrier rocket." />

<center><font size=2px color=grey>The Smart Dragon-1 carrier rocket.</font></center>

&emsp;&emsp;Smart Dragon-1 consists of four solid-propellant stages arranged in series. The rocket is 19.5 meters long and 1.2 meters in diameter, with a liftoff mass of roughly 23.1 tonnes. It is China's smallest and lightest solid-fueled rocket. Tang Yagang, president of China Long March Rocket Co., Ltd., said Smart Dragon-1 can carry 150 kilograms into a Sun-synchronous orbit 700 kilometers above Earth. A single launch costs no more than 25 million yuan, allowing the rocket to meet customers' needs for either “one rocket, one satellite” or “one rocket, multiple satellites” missions.

&emsp;&emsp;China Rocket is not an entirely private institution. It is a subsidiary of the China Academy of Launch Vehicle Technology (CALT), which is itself part of the state-owned China Aerospace Science and Technology Corporation (CASC). CASC is China's principal aerospace contractor and, over the years, has built rocket families such as Shenzhou and Long March.

&emsp;&emsp;According to Xinhua, the company developed Smart Dragon-1 in less than eighteen months. If all goes well, this launch will lay the groundwork for more missions in the future. Smart Dragon-1 is scheduled to launch four more times before the end of 2020.

&emsp;&emsp;Xinhua reported that the mission carried three satellites. The primary payload, Qiansheng-1 01, was developed by Beijing Qiansheng Exploration Technology Co., Ltd. The two rideshare payloads, Xingshidai-5 and Tianqi-2, were developed by Beijing Smart Satellite Technology Co., Ltd. and Beijing Guodian Gaoke Technology Co., Ltd., respectively. Once in orbit, the satellites will carry out a series of technology demonstrations and provide data services.
